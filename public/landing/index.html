Sitting IV · the API, both ways

What CMC made possible, and where it got in the way.

Made possible

  1. quotes/historical: daily supply per wrapper, the engine.lib/cmc/history.ts
  2. v5/real-world-assets: every wrapper of a stock, across 12 issuers.lib/cmc/rwa.ts
  3. credit_count: each lookup’s cost, in its receipts.lib/receipts.ts

In the way

  1. convert=<fiat> on quotes/historical multiplies circulating_supply by the exchange rate; we ask in USD.docs/proof/spike/convert-bug/
  2. One ticker, several ids (NVDA maps to 2): every join is by id.README
  3. /v1/dex/holders/list: 400 for three POST shapes, 405 for a GET; we use the count.docs/proof/spike/

Errata

from the README’s “What we got wrong”
I
We first called supply growth “inflow from holders”. … It is issuer-level creation, so the wording is now “net creation”, counted like an ETF flow.item #1
II
We nearly called NVDAX’s July–August fall a real redemption. … a CMC circulating/total split, not a redemption.item #2
III
Dollars are net creation at market price, not money that changed hands.item #5

Questions a judge would ask

Is net creation money that buyers put in?

No. It is units created minus redeemed, times the market price, counted like an ETF flow table. Every sampled NVDAB mint came from the zero address to one recipient: issuer-level creation, not buyer data.

What does “within remedy” mean?

The Trial of the Pyx checks the Royal Mint’s coins within a legal tolerance, the remedy. Here the chain’s change must match CMC’s within ±10% (or 0.5% of supply for a small move) before REAL INFLOW or OUTFLOW is struck.

Why are 107 wrappers not verifiable?

Their supply, or the move, sits, or may sit, on chains NetMint does not read yet (Solana, Arbitrum, Base, Robinhood Chain, X Layer), and NetMint says so.

What happens if CMC refuses a call?

A call CMC refuses (plan, credits, disabled key) gets a stored dated body, labelled live-stored; beyond those bodies you see CMC’s error. Event access reverts to Basic after 9 Dec; whether that touches this app’s key (Growth-tier limits) is not known. Tested only with simulated refusals.
